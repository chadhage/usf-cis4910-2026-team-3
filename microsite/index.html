02 · Project north star

Build the pipeline—and study how it builds

The software matters. The decision process, evidence, and evolution of the software matter just as much.

Confirmed framing

Lead with an AI-native software engineering pipeline that generates, tests, evaluates, and evolves software from requirements or a formal specification.

Confirmed principle

Evidence over opinion

Use repeatable experiments, working artifacts, test results, decision records, cost data, and explicit limitations to support conclusions.

Core workflow
1 · SpecifyRequirements, personas, backlog, acceptance criteria
2 · GenerateSoftware artifacts and implementation increments
3 · TestUnit, feature, end-to-end, and selected resilience tests
4 · EvaluateRequirements fit, security, operability, and cost
5 · EvolveAdapt implementation and tests as requirements change
03 · Bound the study

Scope & research questions

Compare enough to learn, but not so much that breadth crowds out working software and credible evidence.

Confirmed direction
Proposed for agreement tomorrow

Experiment shape

  • Define the ecosystem universe, then shortlist approximately 3–4 viable candidates.
  • Include the Microsoft ecosystem in the shortlist.
  • Apply each candidate to one bounded scenario; a simple task-management application is the current example.
  • Progress from first iteration through MVP.
  • Consider a non-AI baseline only as a proposed perspective-building experiment.
Scope guardrails
Depth over breadthDo not compare every ecosystem.
Bounded scenarioKeep the experiment comparable and finishable.
Consent firstNo public publication of student work or identity without opt-in.
Protect dataNo secrets or private data in prompts or repositories.
Support claimsNo conclusions without traceable evidence.
Course authorityMilestones and grading requirements remain authoritative.

07 · Ways of working

Sponsor expectations

Practical norms intended to make feedback faster, demos stronger, and research claims more credible.

Proposed for agreement tomorrow
    Confirmed boundary

    Academic and faculty requirements prevail

    AI tools are allowed and desired, but outputs must be validated, usage disclosed, and academic integrity preserved. Student token costs must be considered; no assumption should be made that students personally absorb non-trivial costs.

    08 · Establish rhythm

    Suggested cadence

    A lightweight proposal that prioritizes demonstrations, concise preparation, fast escalation, and continuously captured decisions.

    Proposed for agreement tomorrow
    Meeting hygiene
    Before the sponsor sync
    Student-led agenda and concise pre-read shared 24 hours before, including the demo target, requested decisions, risks, and links to evidence.
    During the sponsor sync
    Lead with the working demo or evidence. Use status only to frame a decision, risk, or request for help.
    After the sponsor sync
    Update the decision log, owners, dates, blockers, and next demonstrable outcome while context is fresh.
    09 · Make choices visible

    Decision log

    Proposed decisions for kickoff. Select an option and capture context; choices remain proposals until the team agrees.

    10 · Surface constraints

    Risk & blocker board

    Starting states are intentionally unresolved. Discuss what is known, name an owner, and avoid fabricating closure.

    11 · Close with clarity

    Decisions, owners, next steps

    Use the final two minutes to read back what changed, what remains open, and what the team will demonstrate next.

    Close-out readback

    1. What did we decide?
    2. Who owns each next action?
    3. What is the due date or checkpoint?
    4. What will be demonstrated next?
    5. Which blocker needs escalation?
